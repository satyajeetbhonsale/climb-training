Prehab routine · 10–12 minutes

4× per week minimum. Best on climbing days and after easy sessions.

IT band complex

  • Side-lying clamshells — 3×15 each
  • Single-leg glute bridges — 3×12 each
  • Side-lying leg raises — 3×15 each
  • Copenhagen plank, knee version — 3×20s each
  • Standing hip hikes — 3×12 each
  • Foam roll TFL and quads, not the IT band itself

Plantar fascia complex

  • Towel scrunches — 2×30s each foot
  • Marble pickups or toe yoga — 2×15 each
  • Calf raises, straight knee — 3×15
  • Calf raises, bent knee soleus — 3×15
  • Eccentric heel drops, 3s lower — 3×10 each
  • Frozen bottle roll — 2 min each, evenings
  • Tibialis raises — 3×15
Morning ritual · 60 seconds, every day. Before getting out of bed, do 20 ankle pumps and 10 toe spreads on each foot. Plantar fascia tightens overnight; first morning steps are when re-injury happens.

Treadmill, stairs, heart-rate zones

Treadmill incline

  • Z2 incline walk: 8–12% @ 5.0–5.5 km/h
  • Sustained uphill: 12–15% @ 4.5–5.0 km/h
  • Hard intervals: 15% @ 5.5–6.0 km/h

Uphill work is always walking pace — loads the posterior chain like hiking and protects IT band and plantar fascia.

Stair machine

  • Z2 steady: level 6–8
  • Tempo: level 9–11
  • Hard: level 12–14

Light touch on rails for balance only — leaning defeats the purpose.

Heart-rate zones

  • Z2: RPE 3–4, full sentences, ~130–150 bpm
  • Z3: RPE 5–6, short phrases, ~150–165 bpm
  • Z4: RPE 7–8, single words, ~165–175 bpm

~80% of training stays in Z2. The discipline is staying easy when it feels too easy.

Recovery basics

Sleep

8 hours minimum, especially Tuesday post-strength and Saturday post-long. If consistently exhausted, cut a session — not sleep.

Nutrition

Carbs around training. Protein 1.6–2.0 g/kg daily. Long efforts: practice 30–60 g carbs/hour with food + gels you can replicate on the mountain. 500 ml water within an hour of finishing.

Red flags

  • Resting HR up >7 bpm for 2+ days → extra rest day
  • Sleep quality dropping despite good hygiene → reduce volume 20%
  • Mood/motivation crash → almost always overtraining; pull back
  • Climbing performance dropping → ease the strength work

Pain management

If pain is sharp, localized, and changes how you move, stop. Muscle fatigue that resolves with warm-up is fine.

IT band

Signs: sharp lateral knee pain, especially descending or after 20–30 min running.

  • Mild tightness → finish easy, prehab + ice 15 min
  • Pain that changes gait → STOP. Stair machine or pool only, 3–5 days
  • Pain at rest or on stairs → 5–7 days off impact, see a physio

Plantar fascia

Signs: sharp heel pain on first morning steps, arch pain after long efforts.

  • Mild morning stiffness → eccentric heel drops, frozen bottle
  • Pain during session → stop, switch to stair machine 3 days
  • Persistent pain → reduce volume 30%, check shoes/heel cup
Boots and pack on your body for every weekend session by Week 3. Blisters and pack-fit issues will ruin the course before fitness gaps will.
